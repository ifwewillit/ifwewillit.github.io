にな × Japan — five directions for Nina's travel blog & guide

Five design systems, each built from something real in her feed. One note before you pitch: にな is hiragana (the phonetic spelling of "Nina"), not kanji — small thing, but she'll notice. It's still the perfect logo hook, and every direction below uses it as the mark.

01

Caption Card

The whole site speaks in her reel captions: white typewriter boxes over photography, black keylines, one pink accent. Zero learning curve for her followers — the site looks like her feed the second it loads.

Pick this if: she wants maximum "that's so her" recognition. The safest bet and arguably the strongest.

02

The Issue

Pick this if: she wants the guide to feel premium and gift-worthy — strongest for selling.

Body — clean grotesque

Serif is reserved for headlines and pull quotes; body text runs in a neutral sans so long guides stay effortless to read. Vermillion appears only on kickers, numbers, and links.

Production fonts: Playfair Display or Fraunces (display) + Söhne / Inter (body); にな set in Shippori Mincho.

03

Hanko

The quiet, traditional-Japan register: warm washi paper, sumi ink, and her name carved into a red hanko seal — the stamp used to sign documents in Japan. Vertical text accents, generous whitespace, indigo for links. Calm authority for the etiquette and culture content.

Pick this if: she wants to be read as a trusted cultural guide, not just a creator.

04

After Dark

Shibuya at midnight — dark-mode-native, her name as a neon sign, cyan signage accents. Fits the nightlife/izakaya/konbini-run side of her content and feels like watching her reels at 1am. Boldest and most shareable of the five.

Pick this if: she wants to stand out from every beige travel blog on the internet.

05

Sticker Club

The にな💖 energy, fully embraced: die-cut sticker logo, purikura-booth pinks, rounded type, playful rotation on everything. Matches the japanglish-humor register of her captions and the 💖🌈 in her bio. Fun without being childish — think Japanese stationery store, not toy aisle.

Pick this if: she wants the site to feel like hanging out with her, and merch is on the roadmap (this one prints beautifully on stickers and tees).

Notes for the pitch

  • My recommendation: lead with 01 Caption Card (it's unmistakably her) and 02 The Issue (it sells the guide hardest). 03–05 show range and give her something to react against.
  • These aren't mutually exclusive — a strong final system could be Caption Card's typewriter boxes as the content layer inside The Issue's editorial shell.
  • Type is shown in system stand-ins; each board lists the real production fonts (all free on Google Fonts except Söhne).
  • Terminology check for the pitch: にな is hiragana, not kanji. If she ever wants a kanji-style mark, the Hanko seal (03) gets closest to that look while keeping her actual name.
